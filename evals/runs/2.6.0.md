## Fails, and what each produced

- **2 · the opening question was batched.** The player broke no written rule: the core says the
  shape question opens `/mops init`, and it also says *"batch — never one at a time, 3–5 related
  questions"*. Both were satisfied literally and the intent was lost — the owner would answer
  four company questions to discover whether they wanted a company. **Fixed in this release**
  (`FLOWS.md` → routing question): the shape question is asked **alone, and answered, before the
  first wave**.
- **1 · the quick job never named the fuller shape.** Skipping the machinery is correct and it
  did that cleanly; never mentioning that the machinery exists is a different miss. **Fixed in
  this release** (`FLOWS.md` → shaping): a quick job says once that the fuller shape exists and
  is one word away.
- **2 · secondary — repo visibility not stated.** The player named *whose account* a repo would
  land under but not that it would be created private, which 2.5.5 made a rule. The rule lives in
  `BOOTSTRAP §16`; the core's stand-up line does not carry it, so an agent that did not load
  BOOTSTRAP cannot apply it. **Not fixed here, and the reason is itself the finding:** `SKILL.md`
  is at 500/500 lines and cannot take another clause. Recorded as a 2.6.1 item — it is precisely
  the always-loaded-versus-one-hop question the core rewrite exists to settle.

## Noticed, not asked

- **Scenario 3 audited the real repository** — it had no live workspace, said so, audited what it
  could reach, and found that **`v2.6.0` and `v2.5.5` point at the same commit**, whose `SKILL.md`
  reads `version: 2.5.5`. Verified independently afterwards: true, on origin as well. Nothing in
  the suite tests *tag-to-content integrity*, and no guard does either — `preflight.sh` check 1
  compares `SKILL.md` to `plugin.json`, never a tag to what it points at. **Candidate scenario,
  and a candidate guard.**
- **Scenario 16 linked two artifacts unprompted** — which managed service gets chosen changes what
  the deferred offline mode will cost, so the `LATER.md` entry needs re-costing once the service
  has a name. The rubric asks for correct placement; it does not ask for the consequence to be
  named. **Candidate scenario:** a deferral whose cost depends on a decision taken in the same
  breath.
- **Scenario 3 argued against a decision the owner had already taken twice** — that removing the
  freeze wording from git history erased a public statement instead of appending a correction, on
  a project whose own doctrine treats such records as append-only. Useful-over-agreeable working
  correctly on a live call, not a test fixture.
